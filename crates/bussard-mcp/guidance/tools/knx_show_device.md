Show what one device can do, in its device file's words, from the lock and the product model under .bussard/model-cache/ (no bus access): its channels (handle, vendor text, the user's name, counts); for an application without channels of its own, its ETS parameter pages (`blocks`, in ETS order); inactive pages and channels marked `inactive` with the parameters that enable them and the objects they would show, and a `summary` counting them. With `channel` (a handle like "a-1", a page handle, or "device" for the device level), that scope's parameters (key, current value, enum choices or range, whether at the vendor default) and objects (key, number, text/function, DPT, flags, what it sends and listens on); for an inactive page, the ones it would show and its full enable chain. A scoped call lists the other pages one line each (handle, text, state, counts); `inactive: true` adds their enable detail as `inactive_pages`. Without a scope, a page whose enable chain repeats an earlier page's lists only its own switch plus `also_needs` (the earlier pages to enable first). This is what the device can do; knx_get_device is the configured state. With `toml: true` also the paste-ready TOML for devices/<address>.toml. Use it before editing a device file: the keys it prints are the ones the file accepts and knx_set_parameter takes (also as `<page or channel handle>.<key>`, `key@reference` or the bare reference), and the unscoped response's `how_to_change` says how to edit and push them (parameters with knx_set_parameter; push with knx_plan_device then knx_apply_device). Without product data it says so, says what to run, and lists what the lock has. For a KNX IP interface, `knxnet` holds its [knxnet] table (`model`: IP assignment, static settings, friendly name, tunnel addresses), what the device last reported (`device`: address in use, MAC, tunnels, state) and where they differ (`drift`). With `live: true` it also reads the device over the bus, as `bussard status <address>` does, and adds `live`: `sentences` (names first; quote them), `mask`, `system_type`, `identity` against bussard.lock, and for a KNX IP interface `knxnet` (IP address in use and how it was assigned, MAC, friendly name, tunnelling addresses, device state), `knxnet_lines`, `model_knxnet` and `knxnet_drift`; `security` on a Data Secure device read with its tool key. The live read is read-only and refused in passive mode; it works for a device the project does not have too (a `knx_find_devices` responder). The ETS ids (`reference` where a key names the parameter, page `id`s, `application`, `computed_by`) and object `flags` are left out; `technical: true` returns them.
